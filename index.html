The age of overlapping crises

A polycrisis is what happens when shocks in separate systems — war, climate, disease, food, politics, finance — hit at once and amplify one another. This map tracks 112 of the century's defining crises and where they converge.

How to read this

Markers are individual crises, placed at their epicentre. Shape and colour show the primary domain; size shows severity (significant, major, catastrophic or systemic). A dashed ring marks a global shock that touched most countries.

Country shading counts how many different crisis domains hit a country in the selected window — a rough measure of concurrence, the defining feature of a polycrisis. Many crises carry secondary domains (Sudan's war is also a food crisis), and those count too. Global shocks add their primary domain to every country when the toggle is on.

This is a curated, illustrative catalogue — not an exhaustive database. Inclusion reflects scale and systemic impact; figures are rounded and many are contested estimates. Data runs through 2025; “ongoing” means still active at the end of 2025.
